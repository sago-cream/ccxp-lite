The UI is coherent, readable, and restrained. The principal remaining opportunities are discovery and information density, rather than a broken visual system.

## Component judgments

**Login.** The purple primary button is unmistakable; labels, input outlines, password visibility, CAPTCHA audio/refresh, recovery link, and secondary destinations are legible and sensibly grouped. The announcement timeline has useful separation from the form. Wide layout keeps a comfortable reading width; narrow layout preserves the complete form and moves announcements below it. Small deductions: on narrow screens the institutional links consume two rows before the login heading; on desktop the three equal-width pill actions make the two secondary destinations more prominent than their likely frequency warrants. These are refinement opportunities, not observed usability failures. The announcement panel clips an entry at its footer, though the explicit “顯示全部公告 (13)” action makes continuation discoverable. Full English translation, help disclosure, validation/error, loading, and submission behavior remain unverified.

**Sidebar.** Neutral navigation and white content separate cleanly. Category icons, chevrons, indentation guides, filled versus outline stars, and a visible active-row tint explain the tree. The empty-favorites instruction is useful. The populated tree is dense: in `home-favorites.png`, the favorites list and expanded personal-data branch repeat several destinations before lower categories, while English wrapping interrupts the otherwise regular row rhythm. This creates more scanning work without making text unreadable. The narrow compact rail preserves content space, but its many similar purple symbols require category recognition without visible labels; the graduation-cap, calendar, clipboard, and folder families are especially dependent on familiarity. Accessible names/tooltips and keyboard access were not observed, so their absence is not alleged. The separate narrow menu-mode capture shows a clear search field and profile trigger, but does not establish the behavior of every rail category or deep branch.

**Expanded profile.** Both desktop and narrow panels have consistent icon columns, aligned control values, a strong preferences/support split, and a distinct logout region. Narrow placement fits within the viewport, with a useful relationship to its top-right trigger. The desktop panel is somewhat tall relative to its small number of controls and obscures a substantial part of the navigation, but grouping remains clear. The outlined navigation-mode value and trailing chevron are understandable; the countdown's adjacent “關閉” text helps explain the small switch. Its text and switch are visually quite compact together, leaving less breathing room than other rows. Mode selection, switch feedback, reset confirmation/feedback, outside-click dismissal, and focus restoration were not exercised.

**Removal dialog.** The title names the exact favorite, the explanation clarifies that removal is reversible, and the red confirmation is distinct from the focused “保留” action. The full-shell scrim and central placement clearly establish modality. Narrow text wraps cleanly; both actions remain side by side with comfortable separation and no clipping. The supplied native full-shell and Tab/Shift+Tab cycling assertions strengthen the keyboard assessment. A shorter action label such as “移除” is a possible stylistic refinement, but “確認移除” is clear and does not need changing. No necessary visible fix identified. Escape, restoration, accessible role/name, and AT announcement were not independently verified.

**Favorites.** Cards give the destination strong 16px prominence with secondary category context and a separately placed favorite star. Desktop columns align, and single-column narrow cards wrap mixed Chinese/English labels without collision. The recent-use section is clearly secondary. Small deductions reflect density and competing entry points: the same favorites occupy both an expanded sidebar and the main card grid, and repeated category subtitles/icons use considerable space when most sample entries share a category. On narrow screens several useful favorites require scrolling; the prominent brand/search area contributes to that distance. These are deliberate spaciousness tradeoffs, not a responsive failure. The filled/outline stars and separate removal dialog give credible state evidence; add, reorder, empty recent-use, and long-title extremes remain unverified.

**Homepage search.** Query, count, result title, category, match emphasis, clear control, and the selected first row form a good hierarchy. The purple border and pale active-result fill are consistent with the suite's focus language. Narrow results wrap legibly, and the popup remains inside the content area. The main deduction is discovery at the bottom: the narrow capture says six results while displaying five, with the sixth available only by scrolling; “捲動查看更多結果” explains the action but does not reveal the next item's edge. The wide capture also places its last item close to that hint. A stronger scroll continuation cue could reduce uncertainty. Heavy matching text and category subtitles are useful, although repeated category labels make a dense group feel busier than necessary. Keyboard selection/navigation, result activation, clear behavior, no-results, asynchronous loading, and AT announcements remain unverified.

## Prioritized feedback

1. **Recommended refinement — search continuation.** Preserve a visible sliver of the next result or introduce a restrained bottom fade/scroll affordance within the result scroller. The count-versus-visible-items relationship in `home-search-narrow.png` is the clearest remaining discoverability issue. Keep the existing textual instruction available.
2. **Recommended refinement — deep navigation scanning.** Consider reducing repeated expanded-tree content or making the current branch easier to distinguish when favorites and several nested groups are open. `home-favorites.png` shows the amount of scanning required. Preserve the useful hierarchy guides and full destination labels.
3. **Optional improvement — compact rail discovery.** If not already available, reveal category names on hover and keyboard focus, and offer a clearly discoverable labeled mode. This follows from the icon-only narrow rail; it is not a claim that accessible names or tooltips are absent.
4. **Optional taste — first-screen density.** Slightly reduce narrow homepage brand/search spacing or offer a denser card presentation for users with many favorites. The current roomy layout is internally consistent and readable; its scroll cost is the tradeoff.
5. **Optional taste — login secondary actions.** Consider giving check-in/out and the separate campus portal less visual area than the primary login. Existing color hierarchy is already clear, so this is not required for release.

No necessary visual fix or release-blocking defect is established by the supplied evidence. Further interaction or accessibility checks should be framed as verification work, not as confirmed defects.
